Demo guide

What to expect at a Cutco demo

A demo should answer your questions, show you how the tools feel, and make the options clearer. It should not require you to buy anything.

The demo, step by step

1. A quick kitchen check-in

Luke asks what you cook, what knives you already use, and what frustrates you. This keeps the demo relevant.

2. The core tools

You see a focused selection of knives and kitchen tools, including how the handles, edges, and common pieces differ.

3. Real demonstrations

Depending on the format, Luke may demonstrate common cutting tasks and explain where each piece earns its place.

4. Your questions

Ask about sharpening, the Forever Guarantee, gifting, service, individual pieces, or sets. Current terms should be confirmed through Cutco.

5. A short recommendation

If you want one, Luke narrows the lineup to the pieces that fit your kitchen and budget. Current prices are confirmed on cutco.com.

6. You decide

You can buy, ask for time, choose one piece, or buy nothing. A useful demo is still useful even when the answer is “not today.”

Before you meet

Three things worth thinking about

  1. What do you cook most often?
  2. Which kitchen task or dull tool annoys you now?
  3. Are you shopping for yourself, a gift, or an existing Cutco collection?

That is enough context. You do not need to clean your kitchen, memorize model names, or prepare to make a decision.

Common questions

How long does it take?

The full experience is about an hour — relaxed, with time to actually talk through what fits instead of rushing. If you already know exactly what you want, text Luke and it takes about 20 minutes.

Can it be virtual?

Yes. Video works well for comparing pieces and asking questions. In-person availability depends on Luke’s location and schedule.

Will I see prices?

Luke can explain options, but prices and promotions change. Confirm current pricing and terms on Cutco’s official website before ordering.

What if I buy nothing?

That is allowed. The goal is to leave knowing what fits, what does not, and what questions to ask later.
